Foundations

D-01 – D-04
D-01 Locked start over

Roguelike deckbuilder, Slay the Spire structure

ChoseDraft cards, climb a generated node map, turn-based card combat, permadeath. Structure deliberately faithful; the class is where the originality goes.
RejectedAction roguelite — animation-hungry in a way a solo dev pays for daily. Turn-based grid roguelike — deep systems, but animation is decoration rather than substance. Tactical — closest runner-up.
WhyA deckbuilder is the one genre where the stated goals don't fight each other. Enemies are static illustrations with short idle loops, so "great animation" means card motion and UI feel — achievable solo — rather than 12-frame run cycles.
D-02 Verified start over

Godot 4.7.2, GDScript

ChoseInstalled and running. Tweens, AnimationTree, GPUParticles2D and shaders all in the box; exports to desktop and web.
RejectedTypeScript + PixiJS — you'd hand-build the animation system, audio bus and particle layer. LÖVE — lovely, but no batteries. Unity — heaviest iteration loop for the least benefit at this scale.
WhyThe engine ships the exact subsystems a juice-heavy card game leans on. Nothing here needed writing from scratch.
D-03 Locked an afternoon

One class now, commercial game as the ambition

ChoseBuild Act 1 with a single class. Architect so classes two and three are content work rather than a rewrite.
WhyEvery card, enemy and encounter lives in JSON and is assembled from composable effects (see D-14, D-16). A second class costs design time, not engineering time.
D-04 Locked an afternoon

No budget until wishlists justify one

ChoseFree and CC0 tooling throughout. Spending gates on roughly 1,000 wishlists off a demo.
WhyIt forces the Phase 1 gate (D-18) to be answered honestly. Money spent before the game is proven fun buys polish on an unproven core.

Art direction

D-05 – D-09
D-05 Amended by D-29 near-total redraw

Pixel art

AmendedD-29 ships a CC0 art pack instead. Hand-drawn remains the target the bible describes and any hand-drawn PNG still wins at load, but it is no longer what ships today.
ChoseHand-drawn pixel art on a locked palette and a locked canvas grid.
RejectedPainted illustration and ink/woodcut — both reproduce far more reliably from AI generation.
NoteRecorded honestly: I argued against this, on the grounds that it pairs badly with an AI-assisted pipeline. You reaffirmed it, so it's the decision — and D-06 resolves the conflict rather than papering over it. The consequence is real: art is now the critical path for every feature.
D-06 Locked a policy change

AI for reference only; every shipped pixel hand-drawn

ChoseAI generates concept boards, silhouettes, composition and colour studies. No AI-generated raster ships, in whole or in part.
RejectedAI-assisted pixel tools (PixelLab, Scenario) — viable, but they'd require Steam disclosure and daily palette policing.
WhyTwo reasons that point the same way. AI output is pseudo-pixel — off-grid, anti-aliased, palette-exploded past 200 colours — so it needs near-total redraw regardless. And Steam's 17 January 2026 rewrite narrowed disclosure to player-facing shipped content, explicitly exempting concept-art ideation whose output doesn't ship, plus AI code assistants. This pipeline therefore needs no AI disclosure on the store page.
D-07 Locked redraw everything

960 × 540 base, integer-scaled

ChoseDoubles to exactly 1920 × 1080. Viewport stretch, aspect keep, integer scaling on, nearest-neighbour filtering — set project-wide.
Rejected640 × 360 — cards land near 64 × 88, forcing a 5px font. Rules text readability is the known killer in pixel card games.
WhyEvery sprite you draw is bound to this number. It is the single most expensive decision on the page to revisit.
D-08 Open rises after art pass

A 32-colour locked palette

AmendedD-29 ships sprites carrying their own palette, so locking emberwright.gpl would now fail every one of them. The rule drops from a conformance gate to a rule for code-drawn UI, where it still holds.
ChoseThirty-two colours, one .gpl file, no exceptions. Ember orange is reserved for the Mana gauge and the Overloaded state — nothing else may use it, so "this is the resource" always reads at a glance.
PendingEndesga-32 recommended; DawnBringer-32 and AxulArt-32 are alternatives. Verify the licence on the individual palette page — Lospec licensing is per-palette, not site-wide.
Enforced bytools/check_art.py fails any sprite containing a colour outside the file. Dimensions-only until the palette lands.
D-09 Locked near-total redraw

Sprite dimensions and the 8px grid

ChoseEnemies 64², elites 96², bosses 128², cards 96 × 132 with an 80 × 56 art window, status icons 16². Everything snaps to 8px.
Also fixedLight source top-left on every asset. 1px outlines in a dark palette colour, never pure black. No dithering below 2px cells. Hue-shift when shading rather than darkening.
WhyConsistency comes from constraints written down before drawing, not discipline in the moment. Inconsistent light direction is more visible than a wrong colour.

This page is built from Endesga-32 — the same ramp D-08 recommends — and holds to the game's own rule: ember orange appears only where something is hot, which here means the Mana mechanic and the decisions still open.

Audio

D-10
D-10 Locked an afternoon

Licensed library now, commissioned composer later

ChosePhase 1 on CC0 and royalty-free sources: Kenney (CC0, no attribution), the Sonniss GDC 2026 bundle (7.47 GB, unlimited projects, lifetime), OpenGameArt and Pixabay filtered to CC0.
RejectedAI-generated music — commercial rights vary sharply by service, it would trigger Steam disclosure, and OST quality is a recurring review complaint. Commissioning now — right call later, wrong call before the game is proven.
Hard rule“Royalty-free” is not “free to embed in a game.” Many standard tiers cap copies or exclude game embedding outright. Every file gets a licence row in assets/audio/LICENSES.md at download time, not later.
First fiveCard draw, card play, hit impact, block gain, intent reveal. Deckbuilders live or die on these.

The class

D-11 – D-12 · D-27 – D-28
D-11 Open content, not code

The Emberwright, built on a Mana curve with Ramp and Overload

ChoseMana is the cost of every card and the class's whole identity. It opens at 1, climbs by one a turn to a cap of 5, and the class breaks that curve two ways: Ramp N permanently raises the refill and grants nothing the turn it is played; Overload N hands you Mana now against a debt that shortens every refill until it is paid off. Cards marked Overloaded: pay a bonus for as long as you carry that debt. The repayment schedule is D-27, which amended this entry.
WhyRamp and Overload pull opposite ways on one dial, so every turn asks the same legible question — spend now, invest for later, or borrow against later? Ramp is strongest on turn one and worthless at the cap; Overload is always available and always charges. Neither is ever the automatic answer. And it is a visual: pips that light, spend and lock, an alarm strike-through sizing next turn's shortfall, embers while in debt.
ArchetypesArrive early (ramp on turn one), Burst and pay (overload into an unanswerable turn), Cash out (vent/temper turn leftover Mana into damage or Block). afterburn bridges the last two by rewarding the act of borrowing rather than the size of the debt; quench is the release valve that makes the burst archetype draftable at all.
Not chosenA Heat gauge — the original proposal: a 0–10 bar with a Stoked threshold at 6 and an Overheat penalty at 10. It was a second resource sitting beside a flat 3 energy. Folding the identity into the resource the game already had removes a bar from the HUD and makes every card cost part of the mechanic rather than a tax paid before it. Retired before it was ever playtested, so nothing was learned from it — but the class has now had two identities and neither has passed D-18.
StatusA proposal, built so there was something to playtest. Your call.
D-12 Locked an afternoon

Baseline numbers

Chose75 HP · Mana 1→5 · draw 5 · starting deck of 3 Strike, 1 Defend, 1 Kindle — five cards. Act 1 enemies 8–34 HP, elites 44–58, boss 120.
WhySpire-adjacent on purpose, so playtest signal is about the Mana curve rather than about tuning noise.
UnresolvedThe enemy numbers were tuned against a flat 3 energy. A curve opening on 1 makes the first two turns markedly harder: autoplay went from 3 of 6 runs reaching the boss to 2 of 6 on the same seeds. Unretuned — this is the first thing to measure.
D-27 Verified a refactor

Overload is a debt that carries interest

ChoseOverload N hands over N Mana and adds N to a debt. Every refill is reduced by the whole outstanding debt, and only one point is written off per turn — so borrowing N costs N + (N−1) + … + 1: 1 costs 1, 2 costs 3, 3 costs 6, 4 costs 10. Overloaded: clauses read the debt, so they stay live for as many turns as it runs.
WhyThe original rule charged the debt once and wiped it — N Mana now for exactly N Mana next turn. That is a loan at nought per cent, and a loan at nought per cent is always worth taking, so the mechanic the class is named for asked no question. Charging interest makes the size of a borrow a real decision, because the cost curves up sharply while the benefit does not.
Knock-onCarrying debt is now a stance you pay rent on rather than a one-turn combo: a debt of 3 keeps every Overloaded: card live for three turns and costs six Mana. That gives quench, slack_tub and quenching_oil a job — writing the debt off also switches the payoffs off. Zero-cost cards stop a deep debt producing a blank turn, which gives ember_jab and flare a role they did not have.
Not chosenPaying down half the debt each turn — gentler, but the interest curve flattens exactly where the interesting risk lives (3 costs 4 instead of 6), and "half, rounded down" is harder to hold in your head mid-turn than "one a turn".
Costlast_ember retuned from Overload 4 (now a ten-Mana bill) to Overload 3. The Mana gauge had to change with it: the debt no longer vanishes when charged, so it draws the refill the debt is eating and states how many turns are left to run.
D-28 Verified a refactor

Enemies are puzzles, expressed as data

ChoseThree levers, all in JSON: opening (a fixed move sequence on the first turns), condition (a move only legal in a visible state of the fight — HP threshold, turn number, whether the player is carrying debt) and passives (effects resolved at the start of its turn regardless of intent). Moves may also carry a tell, a short phrase rendered on the intent line.
WhyEvery Act 1 enemy was one attack move and one block-or-buff move behind a weighted die. Nothing in the roster ever made the player change plan: you read the intent to price your Block and played your best cards in descending order. The enemies that make Act 1 of a deckbuilder memorable each impose a small rule that invalidates the default line — and none of ours did.
The real gapNothing in the roster interacted with Ramp or Overload at all, so the class mechanic was purely self-facing and Ramp was only ever a greed play. Coal Thief drains the refill itself; Damper gates its worst moves on the player carrying debt. Both halves of the curve are now things the fight argues with.
Not chosenHard-coding behaviours in GDScript subclasses — faster for the first enemy, worse for the twentieth. It would also put enemy rules outside the JSON that D-14 says content lives in, and outside the integrity test that now checks every opening names a real move and every condition names one the engine evaluates.

Architecture

D-13 – D-17
D-13 Verified start over

The simulation never awaits the view

ChoseCombat resolves state instantly and synchronously, pushing VisualEvent records onto a queue. The view drains that queue and animates at whatever speed it likes.
WhyThis is the decision that makes "great animations" achievable rather than a bug farm. Interleave the two — await tween inside damage calculation — and you get race conditions, unskippable animations, corrupt saves and untestable combat.
Proof49 assertions run headlessly with no display attached. That's only possible because the rules engine has no nodes in it. Animations can also be sped up or skipped wholesale, which matters on a hundredth run.
WarningRetrofitting this is a rewrite. It's baked in from the first commit for exactly that reason.
D-14 Deviation a converter script

Content in JSON, not Godot .tres resources

Departs fromThe approved plan specified .tres. This is the one place the build diverges from what you signed off.
WhyHand-writing .tres with nested Array[CardEffect] sub-resources is error-prone, unreadable in a diff, and can't be loaded in a headless test without the editor. JSON is reviewable, editable outside Godot, and testable.
Kept openThe runtime objects are still Resources, so a .tres pipeline remains possible later without touching a line of effect code.
D-15 Verified a refactor

Independent named RNG streams

ChoseSeparate generators for map, shuffle, rewards, shop, events, enemy AI and cosmetics — each seeded from a hash of the run seed and the stream name.
WhyShare a stream and drawing one extra card changes the map. Runs stop being reproducible, seeded bug reports become worthless, and daily seeds are impossible. Godot's Array.shuffle() uses the global RNG, so it's banned here.
D-16 Verified a refactor

Cards are arrays of composable effects

ChoseTwelve effect types — damage, block, status, draw, mana, ramp, overload, clear_overload, spend_mana, heal, conditional, add_card — each resolving against a shared context. Overloaded is a conditional wrapper, not a special case in the resolver.
WhyAdding a card is a data edit. A new condition costs one match arm. Enemies run the same effect objects the player does, so combat rules exist in exactly one place.
D-17 Verified an afternoon

An act is 8 stages, read left to right, and the last stage is the boss

ChoseSeven generated stages of seven lanes, plus the boss. Four seeded paths walked forward, drifting at most one lane per step; crossing edges rejected. Stage 0 always Combat, stage 3 always Treasure, stage 6 always Campfire, stage 7 the boss.
WhyShortened so a whole run is playable in one sitting — which is what makes the D-18 fun gate answerable at all, since nobody judges a mechanic they never finished a run with. Four paths rather than six because seven stages at width seven fills nearly every cell and choice degrades into noise. Left to right because a journey reads as a journey, and on a 960×540 frame depth belongs on the long axis.
UnresolvedThe shortening invalidated numbers it did not touch: half the nodes means half the attrition, gold and deck growth, against enemies tuned for the old length. Autoplay went from 2 of 6 runs reaching the boss to 5 of 6, and 2 of 6 killing it. A retuning proposal exists; none of it is applied.
D-24 Verified a refactor

Card text is generated from the effects; the authored copy is a checked mirror

ChoseCardData.describe(upgraded) builds a card's text by asking each effect to describe itself. The authored text in JSON stays, and a test asserts the two agree exactly for all 31 cards at base rank.
WhyAn upgraded card has to be readable before it exists — on the reward screen, and at the campfire where the player is deciding whether to spend the upgrade at all. Generating the text means it cannot disagree with the effects; keeping the authored copy means the prose stays good, and gives the drift test something to pin the generator against.
Not chosenA second authored upgraded_text per card — 31 more strings to keep in sync with numbers that move during tuning, and the failure is silent: the card reads 6 and hits for 9.
DetailBuilding it surfaced a live bug — every card in hand drew its base text, so an upgraded Strike+ read "Deal 6 damage" while dealing 9 — and two cards whose text was wrong rather than vague. "Stoked: deal 3 more" was a separate hit that re-runs the damage pipeline, so with Strength it beats +3 on one hit.

Run loop

D-20 – D-21, D-25
D-20 Verified a save migration

The map is derived from the seed, never serialised

ChoseA save stores the run seed, the current node key, and the RNG stream cursors — not the map itself. The map is regenerated on load.
WhyA stored map can disagree with the generator; a derived one cannot. Storing cursors as well as the seed matters separately: restoring only the seed would rewind every stream to the start of the run, so a reloaded save would re-roll rewards the player has already seen.
Rests onOnly safe because map generation draws from its own RNG stream — see D-15. Round-trip asserted in tests.
D-21 Locked an afternoon

Potions are free, and playable at any point in your turn

ChoseNo Mana cost, no once-per-turn limit. Three slots.
WhyPotions are the deckbuilder's safety valve — the thing that lets a player survive a draw that betrayed them. Charging Mana turns them into slightly worse cards, which removes the reason the category exists. The constraint is slot scarcity, not cost.
D-25 Verified an afternoon

Event choices declare what the run must already have

ChoseA choice may carry a requires block — gold, hp_above, deck_above, upgradeable_card, free_potion_slot, relic. Unmet, the option is shown greyed with its reason.
Whylose_gold takes only what is there, so "Lose 60 gold. Obtain a relic." handed a broke player a free relic — the same hole in three of the eight events. Requirements close it in data rather than as a special case per event, and they stop an event killing anyone: a choice that costs HP is offered only while you can survive it.
DetailEnforced in the rules layer, not only in the screen — a gate the UI owns is only as strong as the screen drawing it. No run state may lock every choice of an event, asserted against a deliberately destitute run rather than trusted.

Presentation

D-22 – D-23
D-22 Verified an afternoon

Placeholder art is generated at runtime, never committed

ChoseSprites are built procedurally in code: a mirrored silhouette on a 16×16 logical grid, upscaled, shaded from a palette ramp with the light top-left, 1px non-black outline. Nothing is written to assets/sprites/.
WhyThe animation layer needed real sprites at the real locked dimensions to animate against, but D-05 says every shipped pixel is hand-drawn. Generating them at runtime keeps both true — a hand-drawn PNG at the expected path is used the moment it exists, with no code change.
Not chosenCommitting generated PNGs — that would put art in the repo the conformance checker would bless and a future reader would mistake for real.
D-23 Locked a refactor

All game feel routes through one autoload, and speed is a player control

ChoseShake, hitstop, flash, pop, lunge and popups all live in src/fx/juice.gd. Playback speed is one multiplier with an in-game 1×/2×/4× toggle.
Why"The game feels mushy" should be a tuning session in one file, not an archaeology expedition through fifty scenes. And on a hundredth run the player wants the animation they loved on run one to get out of the way — only possible because the simulation never waits for the view (D-13).
DetailPlayback timers ignore time_scale, so a hitstop can never stall the event player. Juice.intensity = 0 disables all motion, which doubles as a reduce-motion accessibility setting.

Process

D-18 – D-19
D-18 Open an afternoon

Phase 1 gate: is it fun as rectangles?

ChoseNo art until grey-box combat is fun. The current UI is deliberately buttons and text, and is built to be thrown away in Phase 3.
WhyA deckbuilder that isn't fun as coloured rectangles will not be rescued by pixel art. Finding that out before twelve enemies are drawn is the difference between a redesign and a dead project.
Waiting onA full Act 1 run is now playable end to end. Twenty minutes with it, and a judgement on whether the Mana curve produces an interesting decision every turn.
D-19 Verified an afternoon

The art bible is enforced by tooling, not memory

Chosetools/check_art.py decodes every PNG with the standard library — no dependencies — and fails any sprite with off-palette colours or wrong dimensions.
WhyPalette drift is the documented failure mode of pixel-art projects, and it happens one forgivable exception at a time. A rule a script enforces is a rule; a rule in a document is a hope.
TestedVerified against synthetic conforming and violating PNGs rather than assumed working — it catches both violation types and correctly ignores transparent pixels.
D-26 Verified an afternoon

Dev shortcuts resolve through the real path, and are off unless asked for

ChoseLaunching with --dev adds a "win fight" button. It deals lethal damage to every enemy and lets normal resolution run, rather than assigning Result.VICTORY directly.
WhyThe reason to skip a fight is to test what comes after it. A shortcut that jumped straight to the result would skip the event queue too, so the reward screen would be reached by a route no player ever takes — and the two would drift apart silently the moment the real path changed.
Not chosenEditor-only detection — OS.has_feature("editor") would turn it on inside every test and screenshot run, which is exactly where a hidden win button does the most damage.
D-29 Locked a refactor

Ship a CC0 art pack and a CC0 audio library instead of hand-drawn art

ChoseCreature sprites from Kenney's Tiny Dungeon, sound from Kenney's RPG Audio, text in Kenney Pixel — all CC0 1.0, all human-made. Provenance recorded beside each.
WhyD-05 made art the critical path for every feature and it stayed there: the game still looked like coloured rectangles, so D-18 could not be judged against anything resembling the real thing. D-04 rules out commissioning until wishlists justify it, so "hand-drawn" meant, in practice, "not drawn".
CostD-09's locked sizes survive — the pack is 16×16 and scales by whole numbers to 64, 96 and 128. D-08 does not. The pack carries its own palette and will never conform to Endesga-32, so the palette rule drops to code-drawn UI only.
Not chosenAI-assisted pixel tools — still rejected, now for a second reason: they would require the Steam disclosure a CC0 pack does not, for output that needs redrawing anyway. D-06 and the disclosure position are untouched; every asset here is human-made.
